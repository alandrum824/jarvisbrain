# Park Master Plan

The overall concept and guest-flow layout for [[Promiseland Park]].

## Core Values
Faith, Hope, Grace, Love, Family, Kindness, Forgiveness, Courage, Imagination, Joy, Purpose — see [[Promiseland Park]] for the full vision statement.

## Design Direction
Mid-century family-entertainment warmth combined with modern immersive theme park design. Original IP only — no copying another entertainment company's characters, architecture, or attraction concepts (see [[Brand and IP]]).

## Guest Flow
Guests enter through [[Hope Street]], which leads into [[Promise Plaza]] — the central hub ("Every path begins here"). Promise Plaza connects four lands:
- [[Garden of Grace]]
- [[Faith Harbor]]
- [[Glow Haven]]
- [[Promise Frontier]]

## Affordability
A founding goal: a world-class family experience should not feel out of reach for ordinary families. No ticket price has been established — see [[Financial Model]].

## Founder Vision cross-reference (2026-08-24)
[[Founder Vision]] now governs how every land's storytelling gets designed — faith without superiority, no condemnation-framed attractions, no "us vs. them" storytelling. Reviewed all existing land/attraction canon against it: **no direct contradictions found.** [[Garden of Grace]] and [[Faith Harbor]]'s core messages (grace, "never alone in the storm," "not better than others — called to love them") already align closely, likely because this philosophy was implicit in the storytelling from the start. One genuine watch-item, not a current violation: [[Journey of Grace]]'s Roman-occupation/capture sequence is the single part of the park most at risk of drifting into "us vs. them" or excessive-intensity territory if not executed carefully — it already carries a no-graphic-violence constraint, and should be held to the Founder Vision standard explicitly during real design, not just by the existing constraint.

## Balanced Phase 1 Master Plan (2026-08-24)

Site hunting paused to answer a different question first: **what does PromiseLand actually need to open as a credible family theme park?** The full six-land, full-story vision does not need to open on Day 1. | [[Glow Haven]] | **Partial, lighter nighttime form** | [[Garden of Light]] opens Day 1 as the daytime anchor; a scaled-down nightly lighting/ambiance moment (not the full [[Glow Parade]]) covers the land's nighttime identity until Phase 1B. |

### Hope Street — minimum Phase 1 program
RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED. No shop/restaurant names finalized. Minimum needs: a bakery, a coffee counter, one family sit-down dining option, one or more quick-service points, a general marketplace area, one main merchandise store, a guest services location, restrooms sized to the entry district's crowd concentration, a first aid station, a street-entertainment program (musicians/storytellers, per the land's own theme), shaded seating distributed along the street, and shade structures generally — Southern California sun exposure makes shade a real operational need, not a nicety.

### Promise Plaza — opening-day needs
RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED. Central fountain/water feature (scaled appropriately for Day 1 — the final park icon is explicitly not being decided here, see [[Open Questions]]), clear guest circulation to all connecting lands, a defined meeting-point function (families splitting up need a landmark to reconvene at), shade, seating, a wayfinding system (physical signage, not assumed to be app-based), basic event capability (small gatherings, photo moments, possibly a modest stage/performance spot), and night lighting — the plaza needs to read as alive after dark even before Glow Haven's full nighttime program exists.

### Target visit length
**Recommend Full-Day as the Day 1 target**, with **Full-Day-Plus-Nighttime as the explicit Phase 1B goal** once [[Glow Parade]] launches. RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED. Reasoning: the Day 1 mix (three headline attractions across three lands, all six lands walkable and themed even where lighter, Hope Street dining/shopping, Promise Plaza as a real gathering space, and the supporting-experience program below) is enough to occupy a family from open to late afternoon/early evening. It does not yet include a true nighttime spectacle strong enough to anchor guests staying deliberately past dark — that's what Glow Parade is for, and it's deferred. Do not assume any specific attendance number; see [[Feasibility Study]] for the capacity-input list professional planners will need.

### Future expansion — Phase 1 without a rebuild
RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED, and none of this is assumed to happen. Balanced Phase 1 is designed so growth doesn't require tearing anything down:
- **Additional rides within existing lands** — every land except Hope Street/Promise Plaza opens with only one major attraction, leaving clear room to add a second within the same themed footprint later.
- **New land expansion** — the acreage framework in [[California Site Search]] reserves a Future Expansion bucket specifically so a seventh land or major land extension is physically possible without displacing Phase 1.
- **Hotel** — a common real second-phase move for parks at this scale (see comparable-park research in [[California Site Search]]); not assumed.
- **Entertainment district** — a Hope Street-adjacent dining/shopping expansion beyond the park gate, akin to how larger resorts add a second commercial district over time; not assumed.
- **Additional parking** — scales with attendance growth, not built out to a mature-park size on Day 1.
- **Second gate** — flagged explicitly as a very long-term possibility only, not a Phase 1 or Phase 1B consideration.

Guest flow matches the established sequence exactly: Parking/Arrival → [[Hope Street]] → [[Promise Plaza]] → four lands. The backstage spine runs along the rear/side of the property, out of guest sightlines, with a service spur reaching each land from behind rather than through it — this is what lets delivery trucks, warehousing, and employee movement stay invisible to guests without needing a separate backstage zone per land.

### Hiding the infrastructure
Show buildings for [[Where Are You]], [[Peace Be Still]], and [[Garden of Light]] are placed at the *rear* of their land (against the backstage spine, not against Promise Plaza or another land), so guests approach them through themed landscaping/streetscape rather than seeing a structure first. Berms, dense tree lines, and the themed facades described per-land below are the tools protecting sightlines — the same real Imagineering sightline-testing principle already cited (checking what's visible from guest paths before committing to a building's height/placement).

### Backstage strategy — one shared spine
Rather than a separate back-of-house zone behind each land, **one efficient backstage/service spine** runs along the property's rear boundary, serving all four lands and Hope Street from a single central receiving/warehouse/maintenance cluster. Conceptual zones: central receiving, warehouse, maintenance (general + ride maintenance), food storage, employee facilities, administration, security, waste handling, and utility yards — all clustered together rather than duplicated per land, plus emergency access routed along the same spine for fast reach to any point in the park. This is the real driver behind the smaller back-of-house acreage (~11 ac vs. the ~15–25 ac a per-land-siloed model would need).

### Parking and arrival — phased, not sized to hypothetical attendance
**RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED.** Phase 1 surface parking sized to a realistic (not invented) opening-demand assumption, with required ADA parking built to full code from day one (never the flexible part of this plan). Separate/remote employee lot. Dedicated bus/group drop-off and rideshare pickup/drop-off zones, kept apart from the main lot. A defined entry plaza and security screening point between parking and Hope Street. A reserved parking-expansion area (not built Day 1) allows the lot to grow with real attendance data rather than guessing upfront. No structured parking assumed — surface parking remains the right call at this scale (see [[Financial Model]]).

### Future expansion — protected from Day 1
Concealed expansion pads reserved (not designed) for: [[Journey of Grace]] (Faith Harbor), [[Beyond Tomorrow]] (Promise Frontier), one additional attraction each in Garden of Grace and Glow Haven, expanded food/retail, and parking expansion — plus a larger future-expansion reserve (~48 ac) for a possible second gate, hotel, or entertainment district far in the future. The layout above is built so growth happens *outward from the backstage spine and beyond the four lands*, never by demolishing or reworking Phase 1's Hope Street/Promise Plaza core.

### Ideal property geometry — for the California site search
**This is a size-and-shape brief, not just an acreage number.** For a ~175-ac total property:
- **Approximate dimensions:** roughly **2,400 ft deep × 3,150 ft wide** (~0.45 mi × ~0.6 mi) for a moderately rectangular parcel — depth here means the guest-flow axis from entrance to the rear of the park.
- **Shape:** roughly rectangular to gently irregular, with a depth-to-width ratio near 1 : 1.3, not a long thin strip.
- **Road frontage:** a real, substantial frontage (recommend 800–1,200+ ft minimum) on a significant arterial or highway — not a minor local road. This is directly informed by the access weaknesses already found on real candidates in [[California Site Search]] (Grand Ave/Milan Rd, Nuevo Rd/Menifee Rd) — both are local roads, not the kind of frontage this plan wants.
- **Main entrance orientation:** facing the frontage road most directly connected to the regional highway network, with Hope Street's axis running back into the site's depth.
- **Service-road access:** a *separate* secondary road frontage or easement on the opposite or side boundary from the main guest entrance — essential to keeping the backstage spine's truck/employee traffic entirely away from guest arrival.
- **Guest parking relationship:** positioned between the main entrance and the highway frontage, so guests never cross guest-facing park areas to reach their car; room to expand laterally.
- **Backstage relationship:** positioned along the rear or side boundary, adjacent to the secondary service-road frontage.
- **Expansion direction:** reserved toward the rear/side away from the main entrance, so future growth never requires guests to walk back through Phase 1.

**Parcel shapes to avoid:** long, narrow "shoestring" parcels (can't fit guest depth *and* a separate backstage spine without one intruding on the other); highly irregular/pinched-waist parcels (create dead zones for both sightlines and service routing); parcels with only a single access point on a minor local road (repeats the exact access weakness already flagged on real candidates); parcels with steep cross-slope running diagonally across the intended guest-flow axis (major grading cost, ADA-path complications); parcels bisected by a public road or waterway (a permanent guest-flow and security problem).

### Five things that make this layout feel larger than it is
1. Promise Plaza's controlled sightlines — teasing all four lands without revealing them fully.
2. Garden of Grace's hidden-boundary design (winding paths, berms, rockwork) — guests never see the land's actual edge.
3. Faith Harbor's curved shoreline plus bridges/islands making a compact harbor read as a real waterfront.
4. Hope Street's forced-perspective upper stories and shared-wall density — a short street reads as a real town.
5. The single backstage spine keeping every show building, warehouse, and utility yard entirely out of guest sightlines.

### Five problems a professional master planner would need to solve
1. A real topographic survey and grading plan — this entire layout assumes buildable terrain no actual candidate site has confirmed yet.
2. Precise guest-flow capacity modeling (queue lengths, pathway widths) to confirm this compact acreage actually handles real design-day crowds without bottlenecking — **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED**.
3. Real hydraulic/water engineering — whether the "visually connected" water features can share any infrastructure or must be fully separate systems, and how stormwater integrates without reading as stormwater.
4. Fire/life-safety and emergency-vehicle routing through a deliberately dense, narrow-street layout — Faith Harbor's alleys and courtyards are a real fire-code challenge for that aesthetic.
5. Real backstage logistics engineering — whether one shared spine can actually serve four lands' simultaneous morning deliveries and daily operations without bottlenecking.

### Is $125M still directionally compatible?
**Yes.** This spatial plan didn't change the footprint established in [[Financial Model]] (still ~85 ac guest / ~175 ac total, on target) — it made the concept concrete rather than changing its scope. The $89–207M COMPACT PREMIUM range and the $125M "real breathing room" read both still apply directly. A real professional estimate against this actual spatial plan (see the five problems above) is the next thing that could move this number, not anything found in this pass.

## COMPACT PREMIUM Direction (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION

Adam changed the Phase 1 planning philosophy: not the cheapest starter park, but **the smallest practical footprint capable of delivering a premium, beautiful, highly immersive PromiseLand experience** — concentrating capital into what guests see, hear, smell, touch, ride, eat, explore, and photograph, rather than spreading the park across unnecessary acreage. This is a real philosophy change, not yet logged in [[Decision Log]].

### Real precedent for "compact but world-class"
The earlier acreage research (Knott's 57 ac, Silver Dollar City 61 ac, Legoland California 128 ac, Dollywood 168 ac) already showed Balanced-scale parks don't need to be huge. This pass found even stronger precedent for genuinely compact premium parks: **Tivoli Gardens, Copenhagen — ~20 acres**, one of the world's most famous amusement gardens and the direct inspiration for Walt Disney himself, still operating as a beloved world-class destination nearly 200 years later. **Universal Studios Singapore — ~62 acres**, the smallest Universal park, deliberately dense for its island-resort site, and a genuine global destination. **Islands of Adventure — ~110 acres**, the industry's own textbook example of forced-perspective, sightline-controlled design creating the feeling of a much larger world. **A 70–100-acre guest-facing PromiseLand sits comfortably within real, credible precedent for "compact and world-class" — it is not an experimental target.**

### Real design techniques used to hit this target (not copying any park's architecture, IP, or layout)
Confirmed as real, citable Imagineering/themed-design methodology: **forced perspective** (upper building stories built at a smaller scale than ground level — a real, standard 1 : 5/8 ratio technique — makes structures read as taller/grander than their actual footprint) and **sightline testing** (physically checking what's visible from guest paths, historically done with weather balloons at proposed building heights, to keep backstage hidden and immersion intact). Combined with dense themed streets, shared walls, berms, landscaping, water, narrowing streets opening into reveals, and hidden show buildings, these are the real tools this plan uses to make a compact footprint feel larger and richer than its acreage — RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED for how PromiseLand applies them.

### Per-land COMPACT PREMIUM treatment
All lands stay open Day 1 — Adam's explicit preference — but each is sized to feel complete without needing multiple enormous rides, with expansion pads intentionally hidden for future attractions.

- **[[Hope Street]]:** carries much of the park's real commercial square footage, but as a *dense themed street* — narrow, shared-wall buildings, forced-perspective upper stories — not a spread-out shopping district. Buildings share back-of-house, kitchens, storage, utilities, and service corridors behind the scenes; guests perceive many individual places even though they operate efficiently together.
- **[[Promise Plaza]]:** deliberately kept compact enough to feel alive rather than oversized — fountain/water centerpiece, landscaping, seating, shade, wayfinding, entertainment capability, strong night lighting, with sightlines from the plaza teasing each of the four lands rather than fully revealing them.
- **[[Garden of Grace]]:** creates scale through dense landscaping, large trees, berms, rockwork, waterfalls, winding pathways, elevation changes, hidden views, and winding waterways — the land should feel enormous because guests can't see its boundaries, not because it actually is enormous. [[Where Are You]] anchors it; the attraction and land are recommended to share water/environmental infrastructure where practical (RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED, PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED for actual hydraulic feasibility).
- **[[Faith Harbor]]:** the highest-priority compact-premium test — a dense original waterfront village using narrow streets, alleys, courtyards, docks, market stalls, facades, upper stories, and forced perspective to create the illusion of a much larger world, built from PromiseLand's own original architecture, not copied from any existing themed land. A strategically sized harbor (not a large literal body of water) reads as bigger than it is through curved shoreline, buildings obscuring edges, bridges, islands, rockwork, and boats. [[Peace Be Still]] anchors it; expansion space is protected for [[Journey of Grace]] in a later phase.
- **[[Glow Haven]]:** one environment doing two jobs — [[Garden of Light]] by day, transforming by night toward the eventual [[Glow Parade]] — reusing the same landscaping, water, pathways, architecture, and lighting infrastructure for both, maximizing investment efficiency without looking cheap.
- **[[Promise Frontier]]:** opens Day 1 at the smallest premium footprint that works, so all four PromiseLand themes are represented from opening day, with an expansion pad protected for [[Beyond Tomorrow]] if the full attraction can't economically open immediately. Enough environmental storytelling, food/retail, and smaller interactive elements (all **CONCEPT — NOT YET APPROVED**) to avoid feeling unfinished.

### Sets vs. real buildings strategy
- **REAL OCCUPIED BUILDING:** restaurants, shops, guest services, restrooms, attraction show/mechanical buildings.
- **THEMED FACADE / SHOW SET:** Faith Harbor's upper-story village architecture, Garden of Grace's rockwork/waterfalls, forced-perspective elements generally — immersion, not occupied space.
- **HYBRID:** Hope Street's street buildings — real, occupied ground floor with themed/forced-perspective upper levels, the same real Imagineering technique cited above. The goal throughout is premium theatrical placemaking, not cheap fake scenery — money goes toward what guests actually perceive, not toward unnecessary occupied square footage.

### Water strategy
Water becomes PromiseLand's visual signature without consuming unnecessary land or capital, by connecting [[Garden of Grace]] → [[Promise Plaza]] → [[Faith Harbor]] *visually*, not necessarily as one literal hydraulic system: small winding garden waterways and a waterfall in Garden of Grace, a compact fountain centerpiece in Promise Plaza, and a strategically sized harbor in Faith Harbor (the park's main water feature) made to look larger through curved shoreline, obscuring buildings, bridges, islands, and boats. Estimated actual water surface across the whole guest park: roughly **5–12 acres** — a small fraction of the 70–100-acre guest park — achieving an "abundant water" perception through design technique rather than raw acreage. Water treatment, evaporation, California water-use considerations, and maintenance are **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED** — not engineered here.

### Ticketing / Security / Gates (2026-08-24) — CONCEPT — NOT YET APPROVED

Adam's working concept: roughly 7–10 entry processing positions and roughly 7–10 exit positions — **explicitly not validated**, a starting reference point, not a designed capacity. Real methodology, not just this reference number, should determine the actual count: design-day attendance × a peak-arrival concentration percentage ÷ per-lane throughput = lanes needed. **Entry/security throughput methodology could not be researched this pass** (search capacity exhausted before this topic was reached) — genuinely unresearched, not assumed. **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED** for real throughput figures and lane counts once a design-day attendance number exists (see [[Feasibility Study]] Capacity Inputs).

One real, sourced methodology fact did surface from queue-design research: major theme parks design queues to hold a **minimum of one hour's worth of riders**, typically split across three zones — 20 minutes conditioned indoor, 20 minutes shaded outdoor, 20 minutes unshaded outdoor — at a standard **1.5 linear ft per person** space allocation. This is attraction-queue methodology, not entry-gate methodology specifically, but it's a real, citable planning principle worth carrying into both the entry gates and the attraction queues once real design work starts.

Must include regardless of final lane count: security screening, ticket scanning, bag-inspection technology, stroller/wheelchair access, ADA lanes, group entry, employee access, and emergency egress — none of these are the flexible part of this plan.

### Guest Flow Revision — Hope Street Branch-Offs (2026-08-24) — CONCEPT — NOT YET APPROVED

Adam identified a specific real problem with the pure hub-and-spoke model (Parking → Hope Street → Promise Plaza → four lands, all routed through the plaza): it echoes what he explicitly dislikes about Disneyland's Main Street → Hub layout — every guest, regardless of intent, funnels through one central point before reaching any land, creating congestion between guests trying to move quickly toward attractions and guests browsing/photographing Hope Street itself.

**New working idea:** Hope Street itself carries branch-off points along its length leading directly into [[Promise Frontier]] and [[Garden of Grace]] (not yet specified which other lands), rather than routing 100% of traffic through [[Promise Plaza]] first. This creates two effective circulation modes on the same street — a faster "through" path for guests heading straight to attractions, and a slower "browsing" path for guests window-shopping and taking photos — reducing friction between the two guest types instead of forcing them into the same bottleneck. This is a real, legitimate theme-park circulation pattern (multiple parks use branching guest flow rather than pure hub-and-spoke) — not something to build without real crowd-flow modeling, but a sound direction, not just a preference.

**Formalized branch pattern (2026-08-24):** Entry → Hope Street, with early branches allowing ← [[Garden of Grace]] and → [[Promise Frontier]] directly off the street, while guests who want to shop/eat/photograph continue straight down Hope Street toward Promise Plaza; additional downstream branches may provide access to [[Faith Harbor]] and [[Glow Haven]]. The explicit goal is separating **slow exploration traffic** from **ride-focused/destination traffic** without Hope Street reading as "a freeway interchange" — a real, named design constraint to hold onto as this develops.

**Acreage impact:** this is primarily a *pathing* change within the already-allocated ~85 ac guest park, not a request for more land — the branch paths would physically live within the ~12 ac connective/transition-landscaping bucket already reserved between lands. It may even be a minor net efficiency gain: if Promise Plaza is no longer the sole gateway carrying 100% of guest traffic, it may not need to be sized for full-park peak throughput, only the share of guests still routing through it — not yet quantified, and not a reason to shrink the plaza without real modeling.

**Open tension, not yet resolved:** this changes the previously stated guest-flow sequence (Parking/Arrival → Hope Street → Promise Plaza → four lands) that was treated as fixed in the [[Park Master Plan]] spatial work above — Promise Plaza's whole design rationale ("sightlines teasing each land," "first major reveal") assumed it was the *only* gateway into the four lands. If some lands are reachable directly from Hope Street, Promise Plaza's role shifts from "sole gateway" to "the reveal for whichever lands still route through it" — worth resolving explicitly, not just layering on top of the existing plaza design. **PROFESSIONAL MASTER-PLANNING VALIDATION REQUIRED** for real guest-flow/crowd-capacity modeling before this becomes a committed layout — labeled per Adam's own instruction, since this is exactly the kind of conclusion that needs real crowd-flow expertise, not just reasoning from principles.
