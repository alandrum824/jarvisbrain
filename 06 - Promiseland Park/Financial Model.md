## $100M Relationship to Balanced Phase 1 (2026-08-24)

**Not deciding whether $100M is enough — that requires real professional estimates this note doesn't have.** Instead, sorting the Balanced Phase 1 program (see [[Park Master Plan]], [[Attraction Index]]) by likely capital intensity, so it's clear what's driving cost before any number gets judged. All labels below are **RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED**.

**LOW CAPITAL INTENSITY:** Hope Street shop/dining shells (before fixtures/finishes), Promise Plaza hardscape and landscaping (excluding the central icon feature), basic back-of-house buildings, internal roads, surface (non-structured) parking, the supporting-attraction program (playground, walkthrough, street entertainment infrastructure), [[Garden of Light]] (interactive garden, lower ride-technology).

**MEDIUM CAPITAL INTENSITY:** [[Where Are You]] and [[Peace Be Still]] (mid-scale boat/motion dark rides), Promise Plaza's central fountain/water feature, guest services/security/ticketing infrastructure, stormwater and utility infrastructure, employee facilities.

**HIGH CAPITAL INTENSITY:** [[Journey of Grace]] (explicitly PromiseLand's largest planned attraction — living-town scale, staged sequences, extensive backstage/show infrastructure), [[Glow Parade]] (floats, projection systems, choreographed nightly show — night-spectacular productions are historically one of the most expensive ongoing show investments in the industry), land acquisition plus entitlement/CEQA cost (a real, recurring finding across every site researched in [[California Site Search]] — GPA/Specific Plan/rezone/CEQA processes carry real professional and legal cost and multi-year timelines), and structured parking if a surface lot proves insufficient.

**Five biggest likely cost drivers, in order:**
1. **[[Journey of Grace]]'s scale** — the single largest attraction undertaking described anywhere in the project.
2. **Land acquisition + entitlement path** — every site researched so far needs a GPA, Specific Plan, rezone, and/or CEQA review; this is a real cost and schedule risk, not a formality.
3. **Water-based attraction systems** ([[Where Are You]], [[Peace Be Still]]) — boat-ride mechanical and water-treatment systems are a distinct, non-trivial cost category versus simpler attractions.
4. **Offsite infrastructure extension** — every candidate site researched so far has water/sewer/electrical capacity marked "requires confirmation"; extending service to a rural/agricultural parcel could be a large, currently invisible cost.
5. **Nighttime spectacle production** ([[Glow Parade]]) — floats, projection, and performers are typically both capital-intensive to build and expensive to operate.

**What's needed before the $100M question can be answered credibly:** real order-of-magnitude cost estimates per attraction from a themed attraction design/build firm, a land-cost and entitlement-cost estimate specific to whichever site is eventually chosen, a proper civil/infrastructure estimate, and a staffing/opex model. None of these exist yet — **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED** across the board before this section can move from a sorted list to a real budget.

Every number is labeled: **VERIFIED PUBLIC COST** (a specific sourced figure for a named real project), **INDUSTRY BENCHMARK** (a published range/ratio from a credible trade or construction-cost source), **RESEARCH-BASED RANGE** (this note's own reasoning built from benchmarks above), **PROMISELAND WORKING ASSUMPTION** (a planning placeholder with no direct source), or **PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED** (genuinely unknown). ### Attraction cost research (Day 1 anchors)

**[[Where Are You]] (moderate-scale boat dark ride):** RESEARCH-BASED RANGE **$5–15M**. VERIFIED PUBLIC COST comparables: Timber Mountain Log Ride $3.5M (1969), White Water Landing $3.4M (1982), Shoot the Rapids $10.5M (2010), Phantom Theater: Opening Nightmare $3.5M (2026) — all Wikipedia-sourced. INDUSTRY BENCHMARK: "dark rides with elaborate storytelling and animatronics cost $5M–$50M depending on length" (ThemeParkInsider). No exact match exists for Where Are You's specific scope — PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED for a real quote.

**[[Peace Be Still]] — three options, real relative cost difference found:**
- **Option A (physical boat/water ride + storm effects):** RESEARCH-BASED RANGE **$10–20M** — same cost class as Where Are You plus added storm/wave-effect systems (no isolated public cost found for storm effects specifically — PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED for that add-on).
- **Option B (motion-platform simulator):** RESEARCH-BASED RANGE **$3.5–8.5M** — VERIFIED PUBLIC COST comparables: Thor's Hammer $8.5M, Phantom Theater $3.5M. **Meaningfully cheaper than Option A** — avoids a physical flume/channel, water treatment, and boat-vehicle fleet entirely.
- **Option C (hybrid simulator + walkthrough finale):** RESEARCH-BASED RANGE **$6–14M** — reasoned as a blend of A and B, no independent data point found.
- **Read (not a permanent selection):** Option B best preserves Phase 1 capital control; Option A best preserves the described emotional/physical experience (real storm, real water). Option C is a real middle path worth prototyping before committing.

**[[Garden of Light]] (interactive light/water/music garden):** RESEARCH-BASED RANGE **$3–8M** for a Day-1-appropriate scale. VERIFIED PUBLIC COST bracket: splash-pad/interactive water play $45–$210/sq ft (real municipal examples $41–$128/sq ft); Meow Wolf Denver (enclosed immersive art, not a direct match) ran ~$667/sq ft — flagged as a HIGH-end outlier, not a comparable. No direct public cost exists for an outdoor interactive-lighting garden at this scale — PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED.

**Supporting family ride (carousel/train class):** VERIFIED PUBLIC COST: base ride vehicle $50,000–$500,000+ depending on size (24-seat ~$25K, 36-seat ~$60–67K, premium custom $200–500K+); custom theming adds 15–30% per industry sources. RESEARCH-BASED RANGE including show building/queue/theming environment: **$0.5–3M** total.

### Hope Street cost categories
RESEARCH-BASED RANGE using INDUSTRY BENCHMARK construction rates: SoCal retail $75–200/sq ft, restaurant $200–500/sq ft (SoCal runs 20–40% above national baseline). **Necessary Day 1 scope:** bakery, coffee, quick service, guest services, restrooms, first aid — modest finish level, retail-tier rates. **Deferrable/premium scope:** the full-service family restaurant at premium finish, elaborate street-scene facades, and interior theming detail beyond baseline can be phased or value-engineered without breaking the land's function. Kitchen equipment alone typically runs 40–70% of a restaurant build's budget (INDUSTRY BENCHMARK) — a real, specific lever if the sit-down restaurant is simplified or deferred.

### Promise Plaza cost categories
PROMISELAND WORKING ASSUMPTION (no direct public benchmark found for a themed park centerpiece fountain): central fountain/water feature **$0.5–5M** depending on scale — explicitly not a castle or new icon, per instruction; paving/hardscape, shade, seating, landscaping, lighting, utilities, sound, event capability, and wayfinding are folded into the Buildings/Site work lines below rather than double-counted here.

### Land and site development cost categories
- **Site development (grading/drainage/roads, raw to developed):** INDUSTRY BENCHMARK $50,000–$200,000/acre general commercial range (~$120,000/acre often cited as a base); grading alone $15,800–$44,535/acre. **Flag:** any site with real drainage/flood issues (e.g., the documented Lakeview/Nuevo flood-control area found in [[California Site Search]]) would push toward the high end or beyond.
- **Surface parking:** INDUSTRY BENCHMARK $4,500–$9,500/space complete (paving/drainage/striping/lighting/signage) — roughly 1/5 to 1/10th the cost of structured parking ($24,000–$115,000/space), confirming surface parking is the right Phase 1 call already reflected in [[California Site Search]]'s parking framework.
- **CEQA/entitlement:** INDUSTRY BENCHMARK $100,000–$1,000,000+ and 12–36 months, highly scope- and site-dependent. Real cost-reduction lever found: projects consistent with an already-certified Specific Plan EIR may qualify for reduced CEQA review under Guidelines §15182/15183 — directly relevant if a future site sits inside a plan like SP293/SP342.
- **Utility extension (water/sewer/electrical):** INDUSTRY BENCHMARK, low confidence: water connection $8–40/linear ft, sewer trenching $50–250/linear ft, plus connection/impact fees. EMWD (the confirmed provider for every Riverside County candidate researched) uses an EDU (Equivalent Dwelling Unit) fee structure, not a simple per-foot rate — its actual fee schedule couldn't be retrieved. **PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED.**
- Land acquisition itself, entitlement cost, and utility connection fees remain **PROFESSIONAL ESTIMATE REQUIRED** / site-specific until a real parcel is chosen — no specific property is assumed here.

Contingency is shown as its own explicit line throughout this model — never folded into other numbers.

### Testing the ~$100M working target

- **Scenario A — $100M funds construction with land included:** at LEAN/compact acreage, land is a small fraction of the budget (~$1–3M). $100M minus land leaves ~$97–99M for construction — this lands within the **low-to-mid end of the LEAN range ($47–113M)**, meaning a lean, brand-preserving Phase 1 is genuinely plausible near $100M, with real risk of running past it if parking, infrastructure, or contingency draws land at the high end.
- **Scenario B — $100M funds construction only, land acquired separately:** same read — $100M sits comfortably within the LEAN range's low-to-mid band and is tight but not absurd at the LEAN range's high end.
- **Scenario C — what changes if Balanced Phase 1 materially exceeds $100M (it does: $108–237M):** real, credible scope levers exist without touching safety, accessibility, code compliance, or essential infrastructure — defer one Day-1 attraction (push Garden of Light or the supporting family ride to Phase 1B), use Peace Be Still Option B (simulator) instead of Option A (saves a real $6.5–11.5M vs. the physical water-ride option), reduce guest-facing acreage toward the COMPACT band below, build a leaner Day-1 parking footprint and phase in additional spaces as attendance grows (parking is the single largest scope-sensitive line in this entire model), simplify Hope Street building finishes/footprint, keep back-of-house intentionally small with planned expansion capacity, and phase infrastructure extension rather than building full future capacity on Day 1.

**Answer to "does ~$100M appear broadly plausible, plausible only with aggressive phasing, probably insufficient, or impossible to judge":** **Plausible only with aggressive phasing toward LEAN/COMPACT scope** — not broadly plausible at the current Balanced Phase 1 program as defined, and not probably-insufficient either, since a real, credible lean path lands close to $100M. A confident answer beyond that requires the professional estimates flagged throughout this section — this is a directional read, not a verdict.

### Testing $100M / $125M / $150M (land excluded first, then included)

**$100M (excl. land):** sits near the **low end** of the COMPACT PREMIUM range — achievable, but requires holding the lean side of nearly every line: [[Peace Be Still]] as the motion-simulator or hybrid option (not the full physical water ride), a genuinely modest Day-1 parking lot, efficient shared-backstage Hope Street construction, and [[Promise Frontier]] opening very light. **What opens:** all six areas (Hope Street, Promise Plaza, all four lands), all three Day-1 headline attractions, real premium theming concentrated on foreground/sightline elements, modest but real water features (small harbor, garden streams, fountain), Glow Haven's day/night dual-use space (without the full Glow Parade). **What's deferred:** [[Journey of Grace]] (already Phase 2), full [[Glow Parade]] spectacle (Phase 1B), [[Beyond Tomorrow]]'s full attraction (expansion pad protected, lighter experience opens instead). Land included: at this compact scale, land (~$1–4M) barely changes the picture — $100M still functions as approximately a construction-only budget in practical terms.

**$125M:** comfortably covers the COMPACT PREMIUM mid-range and buys real, feelable upgrades: room to move [[Peace Be Still]] to the hybrid option for a stronger real-water/real-motion emotional experience, richer show-building detail and animatronics for [[Where Are You]], denser Faith Harbor waterfront architecture (more buildings, richer facades within the same footprint), an expanded Day-1 parking allocation to reduce early operational strain, and — notably — **mature landscaping at opening** rather than young plantings that take years to fill in (a real, specific "money the guest can feel" lever, see below).

**$150M:** reaches the upper end of the COMPACT PREMIUM range. Real additional capability: potentially the full physical-water Option A for [[Peace Be Still]] while still holding the compact footprint, a fuller [[Promise Frontier]] opening closer to a real headline attraction rather than placeholder experiences, possibly pulling a lighter version of [[Glow Parade]] forward into Day 1 instead of waiting for Phase 1B, a larger contingency cushion (materially lower overrun risk), and back-of-house built to full Day-1 capacity rather than planned-expansion-only.

**Honest read:** $100M is genuinely achievable for a compact, premium PromiseLand — but only near the disciplined end of the range, not with margin to spare. $125–150M is where the "feels expensive" ambition gets real breathing room. None of these scenarios is forced to work — if real professional quotes come back above this range, that's the honest answer, not a reason to compress the numbers further.

### Best Peace Be Still implementation for premium-per-dollar
**Working recommendation (not final): the hybrid option (C).** A real physical boarding/launch sequence with genuine water movement — which reads as premium because it *is* real, physical, and tactile — transitioning to a simulator or walkthrough finale for the technically complex storm sequence. This captures the emotional core of "you were never alone in the storm" through real physical presence at the moments guests feel it most, without paying for a complete physical storm-effects system throughout. Physical Option A remains the strongest pure emotional experience if $150M-tier capital is available; the pure simulator (Option B) is the most capital-efficient but risks reading as less premium than PromiseLand's new direction wants.

### MONEY THE GUEST CAN FEEL
Investments that create the greatest perceived quality per dollar, in priority order:
1. **Landscaping maturity** — mature trees and plantings at opening vs. saplings that take years to read as "established." One of the highest perceived-quality-per-dollar levers in themed design.
2. **Water presence** — the curved-shoreline harbor, garden waterways, waterfall, and fountain described in [[Park Master Plan]] — a small acreage of water, deliberately designed to look abundant.
3. **Lighting design** — especially Glow Haven's day-to-night transformation; lighting is comparatively cheap relative to construction but drives a huge share of guests' emotional read of a space after dark.
4. **Architectural facade detail and forced perspective** — the real Imagineering technique cited in [[Park Master Plan]] (upper stories at reduced scale) reads as premium scale without paying for full-scale occupied square footage.
5. **Show quality inside the three headline attractions** — audio, show sets, and theming density inside [[Where Are You]], [[Peace Be Still]], and [[Garden of Light]] matter as much to guest perception as the ride mechanism itself.

### MONEY THE GUEST DOESN'T SEE BUT CANNOT CUT
1. Fire/life safety systems
2. ADA/accessibility infrastructure
3. Stormwater/drainage engineering (a real, documented regional risk per [[California Site Search]]'s flood-control findings)
4. Structural and ride engineering/inspection
5. Utility backbone capacity (water/sewer/electrical)

This distinction is the actual value-engineering guide: cut or defer scope, never cut what's on this second list.

### Parking — revisited, phased strategy
Not building parking for an attendance level PromiseLand hasn't established. **RECOMMENDATION — NOT YET APPROVED:** a modest Day-1 surface lot sized to a realistic (not invented) opening-demand assumption, a separate/remote employee lot, dedicated rideshare and bus/group drop-off zones planned from Day 1 even though the main lot starts modest, and a reserved adjacent expansion area for additional parking as attendance grows. No structured parking assumed. Required ADA parking capacity and accessibility are never treated as the flexible part of this plan — only the *surplus* general-guest capacity is phased.

## Promise Promenade + Structured Parking Model (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION

Full re-run of the capital model incorporating [[Promise Promenade]] (new non-ticketed commercial district) and real structured-parking research, per Adam's request to "run the numbers again." All figures order-of-magnitude, labeled by source type. Nothing here is approved canon.

### Structured parking, costed at three scales — INDUSTRY BENCHMARK
Design ratios: ~320–350 sq ft/space gross, 10'6"–12'0" floor-to-floor, structures commonly 5–8 levels, structured parking yields 3–5x the spaces of surface parking on the same land. Above-grade structured construction: **$24,000–$48,000/space** (UCLA research average: $52,000/space, land excluded); Inland Empire sits at the lower end of California's cost spread.

| | 500 spaces | 750 spaces | 1,000 spaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross sq ft (~330 sf/space) | 165,000 | 247,500 | 330,000 |
| Footprint (working assumption) | ~1.0 ac | ~1.1 ac | ~1.1 ac |
| Levels (working assumption) | ~4 | ~5 | ~7 |
| **Construction cost** | **$12M–$26M** | **$18M–$39M** | **$24M–$52M** |
| Equivalent surface-lot cost | $2.25M–$4.75M | $3.4M–$7.1M | $4.5M–$9.5M |
| Equivalent surface footprint | ~3.6–4.2 ac | ~5.4–6.3 ac | ~7.1–8.3 ac |

**Structured runs ~5–6x the cost of surface parking at every scale, for ~3–4x less land.** Code/infrastructure notes: ADA-accessible and ADA-EV-capable stalls are *separate* requirements (can't double up); 2025 CALGreen requires 20% of spaces EV-capable (conduit) at 201+ space nonresidential structures, with 50–75% of those needing actual EVCS depending on occupancy type (**PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED** to confirm which tier applies); fire sprinklers required throughout any enclosed garage over 5,000 sq ft (true at all three scales, likely already embedded in the per-space benchmark); elevator/stair core ratios have **NO PUBLIC DATA FOUND** — working assumption ~1 core per 150–200 spaces, PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED.

**Working recommendation: a 750-space structure** as the premium arrival-experience anchor (see [[Park Master Plan]]), supplemented by surface parking for remaining Phase 1 demand, a separate employee surface lot, rideshare/bus zones, and a reserved future-parking-expansion area — not a full 1,000-space structure, which would run disproportionately large relative to the park's own construction budget.

### Promise Promenade construction cost (MEDIUM scenario, ~60,000 sq ft) — RESEARCH-BASED RANGE
Using the retail ($75–200/sf) and restaurant ($200–500/sf) construction benchmarks already established, blended for a premium/architecturally-controlled mix (not generic): **$180–320/sf → $10.8M–$19.2M for buildings** if PromiseLand funds them directly, plus site work/landscaping/plaza/fountain/common areas (~6–10 ac at $120–200K/ac) ≈ **$0.7M–$2M**. If PromiseLand instead builds shells only and tenants fund their own interiors (Model C from [[Promise Promenade]]), shell-only construction runs roughly 60–70% of full turnkey cost (**≈$6.5M–$13.4M**), with tenants separately funding $10–480/SF of their own tenant improvements — real capital, just not PromiseLand's.

### MODEL 1 — Everything inside PromiseLand capital
Land (~$3.3M, using the [[California Site Search]] Linderman Rd example, not a commitment) + Park CAPEX (COMPACT PREMIUM $89–207M, already includes its own soft costs/contingency/pre-opening) + Parking (750-space structure + supplemental surface, $23–53M) + Promise Promenade (PromiseLand-funded, MEDIUM, $11.5–21.2M) + incremental soft costs/contingency on the parking+promenade scope not already covered by the park's own line (~25% × ($23–53M + $11.5–21.2M) ≈ $8.6M–$18.6M) + incremental pre-opening for the promenade (~$1M–$3M).

**MODEL 1 TOTAL CAPITAL REQUIREMENT: ≈$136M–$306M, land included, everything on PromiseLand's own balance sheet.**

### MODEL 2 — Park capital + separate commercial development
**PromiseLand Park capital (retained):** Land (~$3.3M) + Park CAPEX ($89–207M) + a baseline surface-only parking allotment sized to park-guest-only demand (~500–750 surface spaces, $2.25M–$7.1M) + arrival/gate infrastructure (ticketing/security building, gathering forecourt hardscape — $3M–$8M) + incremental soft costs/contingency on the retained scope (~20% × that incremental hard cost ≈ $1.05M–$3M).

**MODEL 2 PromiseLand-side total: ≈$99M–$228M.**

**Commercial development capital (separate, potentially third-party/developer-financed):** the 750-space structure ($18M–$39M) + Promise Promenade buildings ($11.5M–$21.2M) = **≈$29.5M–$60.2M that could potentially shift outside the core theme-park development budget** — the direct answer to whether creating Promise Promenade as a separate asset protects park-focused capital. **Third-party financing is not guaranteed** — this is a real possible structure with real industry precedent (see [[Promise Promenade]]'s Glendale Entertainment District example and financing-tool list: TIF, infrastructure bonds, opportunity zones, special assessment districts, blended CMBS/insurance loans, ground-lease/profit-share arrangements), not a claim that a developer partner already exists.

### SCENARIO A/B/C — Promenade funding structures
- **A — PromiseLand funds it:** full $11.5M–$21.2M buildings + $0.7M–$2M site work is PromiseLand capital. Future upside: PromiseLand keeps 100% of ground rent, base rent, and percentage rent once tenants are in place — the biggest long-term income potential, at the highest upfront risk.
- **B — Third-party developer funds most of it:** developer finances/builds Promenade buildings and (potentially) the parking structure, per the real Master Commercial Developer precedent in [[Promise Promenade]]; PromiseLand retains control through the architectural design standards and master planning, not through ownership of every building. Reduces PromiseLand's upfront CAPEX by the full $29.5M–$60.2M range above, at the cost of shared long-term revenue (ground rent instead of full retail/restaurant income).
- **C — Hybrid/tenant partnership:** PromiseLand funds core infrastructure, common areas, the arrival experience, and key architecture (the parts that most directly serve "first chapter of PromiseLand" immersion); tenants/developers fund individual buildings, tenant improvements, and selected pads. This is the most realistic middle path given real industry precedent — most large entertainment districts researched use some version of this split, not a pure A or B.

### Testing $100M / $125M / $150M PARK capital (land/parking/promenade shown separately)
At any of the three park-capital tiers, the same structural finding holds under **Model 2**: the park-capital number stays focused on attractions, landscaping, water, architecture, and lighting, while land (~$3.3M), a baseline surface parking allotment (~$2.25M–$7.1M), and gate infrastructure (~$3M–$8M) are the only arrival-related costs that stay inside it — total incremental arrival cost inside the park budget: **~$8.6M–$18.4M**, regardless of which of the three park-capital tiers Adam targets. The structured garage ($18M–$39M) and Promenade buildings ($11.5M–$21.2M) sit outside all three tiers under Model 2/Scenario B or C.

**Does ~$125M still work as the core PARK construction target?** **Yes — and Promise Promenade's separation makes it more achievable, not less**, specifically because the two most capital-intensive arrival additions (the garage and the Promenade buildings) don't have to compete with attractions/theming/landscaping for the same $125M if a developer or hybrid structure funds them. $125M still answers "what does the ticketed park itself need," not "what does the whole arrival/promenade/parking vision cost in total" — those are now two different, honestly separated questions.

### Approximate all-in development range
Combining land + park + parking + Promenade, regardless of who ultimately funds which piece: **≈$99M (Model 2, park-capital-only slice, low end) to ≈$306M (Model 1, everything on PromiseLand's balance sheet, high end).** The two-model structure is the real lever for how much of that sits on PromiseLand's own books.

### Five largest financial risks
1. Structured parking wrong-sizing — 500 vs. 1,000 spaces is a $12–26M swing before even hitting the high end of either bracket.
2. Promise Promenade tenant lease-up risk — if Model B/C's third-party financing depends on signed tenants that don't materialize, the capital-shifting benefit evaporates and PromiseLand may need to backstop it.
3. Land acquisition + entitlement risk, now compounded — Promise Promenade likely needs its own separate entitlement path as non-ticketed commercial use, potentially different zoning treatment than the park itself.
4. This entire model is still AACE Class 5 (concept stage, −50%/+100% accuracy) — adding two major new scope items (garage + Promenade) multiplies the dollar exposure of that uncertainty band.
5. CAM/percentage-rent revenue assumptions aren't yet grounded in Inland Empire-specific data (a real research gap this pass) — risk of over-optimistic Promenade revenue projections later.

### Five largest opportunities
1. Shifting $29.5M–$60.2M+ of capital outside the core park budget via third-party/developer financing directly protects the $125M park-focused target.
2. Promise Promenade generates revenue independent of park attendance or ticket sales — a genuine second revenue stream, including from guests who never buy a park ticket.
3. The immersive arrival walkway is a real "money the guest can feel" investment starting the emotional experience before the gate — a potential real differentiator.
4. Ground-lease/percentage-rent structures could produce a long-term, relatively passive income stream once tenants are established — real estate economics layered on top of theme-park economics.
5. Real industry precedent (Glendale Entertainment District-style master-developer models) proves this two-project structure is a normal, financeable pattern, not a novel risk.
