## Tiering: Vision vs. Assumption vs. Verified Fact
Every claim in this project should sit in exactly one of these tiers:

- **VISION** — the values, feel, and story direction ([[Promiseland Park]], the lands, the attractions). Not a factual claim, doesn't need validation, just approval.
- **ASSUMPTION** — a placeholder used for planning (e.g. the $100M figure in [[Financial Model]]). Explicitly labeled WORKING ASSUMPTION — NOT VALIDATED until real data replaces it.
- **VERIFIED FACT** — confirmed by a professional source (market study, appraisal, engineer, attorney). Nothing in this project is in this tier yet.

## Site Requirements — research in progress (2026-08-24)
Two real candidate parcels (Winchester 208 ac, Nuevo 106 ac) now have a public-sources deep dive AND a parcel-level verification pass in [[California Site Search]] covering zoning, entitlement conceivability, utilities, topography, flood, fire, and neighbor compatibility. This moves "Site requirements" from pure VISION into partial ASSUMPTION territory. One piece is closer to VERIFIED FACT: Nuevo's General Plan designation (AG/Agricultural, Lakeview/Nuevo Area Plan) is sourced directly from official Riverside County planning documents — but even that isn't confirmed against the exact parcel, since neither site's APN could be confirmed with the tools available. Notably, Nuevo's documented area-level flood-control issue (Riverside County Flood Control District's active Lakeview/Nuevo Project) now has a plausible-but-unconfirmed reason for optimism (the flood infrastructure appears centered on old-town Nuevo, not this parcel's intersection), and Winchester turned up a real positive (K1 Circuit, a coexisting commercial entertainment venue on the same road) alongside a new concern (the listing status is genuinely unclear — conflicting evidence on whether it's active or off-market). Both original candidates are rated PAUSE, not resolved.

**Pool widened 2026-08-24:** 10 additional properties screened across a broader Southern California search, tiered A/B/C in [[California Site Search]]. Confirms a regional pattern worth carrying into any future feasibility work: no amusement/entertainment-use zoning precedent has turned up organically anywhere researched so far — entitlement risk (GPA/Specific Plan/CUP needed from a rural or agricultural baseline) looks like a regional constant, not a property-specific flaw. Also confirmed the "cheap price ≠ strong site" principle a second time (very low $/acre tracked with a likely-unbuildable mountain parcel; real highway frontage commanded a real price premium elsewhere).

**Price check on the leading Tier A candidate (2026-08-24):** a dedicated pass to confirm the 326.61-ac Winchester ranch's asking price came back unconfirmed despite real effort (13 search variations, 6 direct-fetch attempts). One genuine new lead surfaced instead: **Winchester Hills Specific Plan No. 293**, a real, active 2,840.7-acre master-planned entitlement with a 120-lot tract map approved by Riverside County in June 2026.

**SP293 boundary check (2026-08-24):** the hoped-for entitlement lead didn't pan out as a positive. SP293 BOUNDARY RELATIONSHIP NOT CONFIRMED, and real evidence (SP293's documented boundary, "Case Rd" repeatedly tied to Menifee 92585 rather than Winchester 92596) points toward this parcel sitting outside or at the plan's western edge — and puts the parcel's own city/jurisdiction in question. The June 2026 tract approval is confirmed to be a different sub-area of SP293, unrelated to this candidate's APNs. This is a useful example of a "vision"-stage hope (a nearby entitlement precedent) not surviving contact with the actual governing document — the candidate moved from Tier A to Tier B in [[California Site Search]] as a result, not archived.

## Capacity Inputs for Professional Planners (2026-08-24)

Feeds the Balanced Phase 1 Master Plan in [[Park Master Plan]] and the acreage/parking framework in [[California Site Search]]. These are the inputs professional planners will eventually need to calculate — **no final values are assigned here, and none should be inferred from this list.** All marked **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED**:

- Design-day attendance
- Attraction throughput (riders/guests per hour, per attraction — see [[Attraction Index]] for the Balanced Phase 1 lineup this would apply to). **Partial methodology now sourced:** queues are designed for a minimum 1-hour capacity, split 20/20/20 min across conditioned-indoor/shaded-outdoor/unshaded-outdoor zones, at 1.5 linear ft/person — a real starting standard, not yet applied to specific PromiseLand attractions.
- Entry/security gate throughput (guests/hour/lane) — still genuinely unresearched, not just unvalidated.
- Restaurant seats (by meal-period turnover)
- Restroom demand (fixture counts per code, plus family/accessibility considerations)
- Pathway widths (per guest-density planning standards)
- Queue space per attraction (including shade/weather protection)
- Parking space count (see the Parking and Arrival framework in [[California Site Search]] — categories are defined there, counts are not)
- Egress/emergency evacuation capacity and timing
- Staffing (FTE counts per attraction, F&B, retail, and operations)

## COMPACT PREMIUM Direction (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION
Adam changed the planning philosophy toward the smallest footprint that delivers a premium experience, not the cheapest starter park — see [[Park Master Plan]] and [[Financial Model]]. This meaningfully de-risks the feasibility picture: a smaller total property (~130–220 ac vs. the earlier ~215–350 ac) reduces land cost exposure, site-development cost, and the entitlement footprint a jurisdiction has to evaluate — real support for the "PROPERTY AVAILABLE vs. PROPERTY SUITABLE" distinction already tracked in [[California Site Search]], since more of the real candidate pool (including several previously screened as too small) may now be worth revisiting.

## Founder Vision — Operational Implications (2026-08-24)
[[Founder Vision]] adds real future feasibility/operational scope beyond attractions and construction: a hospitality/employee-culture philosophy (guests never treated as conversion projects, employees never debate religion/politics/sexuality/culture while working, hospitality-first training) that will eventually need real staffing/training design, and a reputational-risk dimension (backlash from multiple directions is expected and should not trigger reactive direction changes). **LEGAL / HR PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED** before any employment policy is built from this philosophy. Nothing here changes capital costs or the checklist above — it's a new, real category of Phase 1 planning (culture/training/reputation) that doesn't yet have its own note or budget line.

## Promise Promenade Feasibility Note (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION
A new non-ticketed commercial district ([[Promise Promenade]]) is now part of the arrival concept, tested against real commercial-real-estate benchmarks (ground lease, build-to-suit, TI allowance, percentage rent — all MARKET BENCHMARK tier) rather than theme-park-specific sources, since it's a genuinely different asset class than the ticketed park. This is a real feasibility positive: commercial retail/restaurant real estate is a far more standard, quotable, and financeable asset class than themed attractions, meaning real developer conversations could realistically happen sooner than themed-entertainment-specific ones. It also adds a real new feasibility question: Promise Promenade likely needs its own entitlement path (non-ticketed commercial use) separate from the park's — not yet researched for any candidate site.

One real methodology finding feeds Capacity Inputs directly: theme park queues are designed to hold a minimum of one hour's worth of riders, split across three zones (20 min conditioned indoor / 20 min shaded outdoor / 20 min unshaded outdoor) at 1.5 linear ft/person — a real, citable standard, now added to the Capacity Inputs list below as sourced methodology rather than left purely as "professional validation required." Entry/security gate throughput methodology specifically remains unresearched this pass (search capacity exhausted) — genuinely open, not assumed.

## Master Planner Problem List (2026-08-24)
Five problems a professional theme-park master planner would need to solve before the first real spatial master plan in [[Park Master Plan]] becomes buildable — all **PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED**: (1) a real topographic survey and grading plan, since the layout assumes buildable terrain no actual candidate site has confirmed; (2) precise guest-flow capacity modeling to confirm the compact acreage handles real design-day crowds without bottlenecking; (3) real hydraulic/water engineering for the visually-connected-but-possibly-separate water systems; (4) fire/life-safety and emergency-vehicle routing through a deliberately dense, narrow-street layout; (5) real backstage logistics engineering for whether one shared spine can serve four lands' simultaneous operations.
