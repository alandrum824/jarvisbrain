## Development Logic Draft (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION, NOT FINALIZED

A first working draft of the investment logic, following Adam's new COMPACT PREMIUM planning direction (see [[Park Master Plan]] and [[Financial Model]]) — begun, not finalized, and explicitly PromiseLand's own story rather than modeled on any other founder's historical pitch:

*PromiseLand starts intentionally compact and premium. Instead of spending capital making the park geographically enormous, Phase 1 concentrates investment into immersive environments and memorable attractions — real design techniques (dense themed streets, forced perspective, shared backstage infrastructure) that make a ~70–100-acre guest-facing park feel like a much larger world, without the capital cost of actually building one. The master plan reserves real expansion pads within existing lands and adjacent land for future growth from Day 1 — this isn't a stripped-down starter park that gets rebuilt later, it's a complete park designed to grow. Guests experience the full PromiseLand idea — all four lands, all six areas — on opening day. Investors see a project sized to real, credible comparables (world-class parks as compact as 20–110 acres already exist and thrive) with a clear, protected path to Phase 1B and Phase 2 rather than an open-ended construction risk.*

## Two-Project Structure (2026-08-24) — FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION

[[Promise Promenade]] introduces a real possibility: PromiseLand may consist of two economically related but distinct projects.

- **PromiseLand Park** — a ticketed themed-entertainment business.
- **Promise Promenade** — non-ticketed commercial/entertainment real estate (dining, retail, entertainment), financeable as a more conventional CRE asset class.

These could eventually involve separate financing, separate development partners, different lenders (theme-park development debt/equity typically prices very differently than retail/restaurant CRE debt), different equity investors, and joint infrastructure agreements (shared parking, shared utilities, shared arrival sequence) between the two. Real industry precedent exists for this kind of split (see [[Promise Promenade]]'s Glendale Entertainment District example and financing-tool list). **This is not legal, tax, or financial structuring advice — LEGAL / TAX / FINANCIAL PROFESSIONAL REQUIRED** before treating any specific entity structure, financing split, or partner arrangement as real. See [[Financial Model]] for the two capital models (Model 1: everything on PromiseLand's balance sheet, ~$136M–$306M; Model 2: PromiseLand-side only, ~$99M–$228M, with ~$29.5M–$60.2M potentially shifted to separate commercial development capital).

**Does this materially improve the investment story? Yes.** It gives investors two distinct assets with different risk profiles — a ticketed entertainment business investors underwrite on attendance/attraction risk, and comparable commercial real estate investors underwrite on lease/tenant risk — a plausible path to reduce PromiseLand's own upfront CAPEX exposure by tens of millions, and a revenue stream independent of park attendance. A real structural improvement, not just a bigger footprint.
