# PromiseLand Park — Founder Vision

Adam's foundational philosophy for *why* PromiseLand exists and *what kind of place* it must become — not a financial decision or attraction concept, but the compass that should guide design, writing, staffing, marketing, and how the organization responds when criticism comes. This is a faith, service, hospitality, and family philosophy — **explicitly not a political position, and "Christian" is not to be read as belonging to any political party or ideological movement.**

## Faith, Service & Unity

PromiseLand Park is openly Christian — Adam does not want that disguised. The stories of Jesus, grace, forgiveness, redemption, sacrifice, hope, love, family, courage, and serving others are foundational to the park.

But PromiseLand is not being built for Christians to separate themselves from everyone else. Adam's philosophy: *we may strongly disagree with each other and still treat each other like human beings.* He does not need to win every argument, does not need to control another person, and does not need everyone entering PromiseLand to believe what he believes. But as a Christian, he believes he is called to serve people, love people, and share what he believes — and PromiseLand should reflect that.

## Everyone Should Be Welcome

Every guest — Christian, atheist, agnostic, LGBTQ+, another religion, skeptical, unsure, or just there for a family vacation — should feel genuinely welcome, not merely tolerated as a potential conversion project. They are human beings and guests in PromiseLand's home, treated with dignity on that basis alone. Adam's own view: God can work in someone's heart through experiences, relationships, stories, and love — but that is between that person and God. **Employees should not treat guests as conversion projects. The organization's responsibility is hospitality.**

## Serve Before We Argue

A core operating principle: **serve before we argue.** PromiseLand demonstrates its faith through what it *does* — treating families well, helping people, creating meaningful family experiences, employing people with dignity, excellent hospitality, supporting communities, generosity, kindness, forgiveness, helping families spend time together, caring for children and elderly guests, accommodating disability, treating employees fairly, and helping people when something goes wrong. **Christianity is experienced through the character of the organization, not just Christian imagery or narration.**

## Faith Without Superiority

PromiseLand should never communicate "we are Christians, therefore we are better than you." The message is closer to: *"Following Jesus calls us to love and serve people."* This is especially load-bearing for [[Journey of Grace]], whose central idea must remain: **following Jesus does not make us better than other people — it calls us to love them.**

## Conviction + Love — the internal framework

PromiseLand does not abandon Christian conviction to welcome people — but conviction is never an excuse for cruelty, humiliation, or disrespect. Internal founder principles (not necessarily public slogans, unless Adam later approves them for public use):

- **Faith without superiority**
- **Conviction without cruelty**
- **Truth without losing love**
- **Service before argument**
- **Every person deserves dignity**
- **The door stays open**

## Unity Without Forced Agreement

PromiseLand doesn't need to solve society's disagreements, and doesn't need everyone to agree. It should demonstrate that people who disagree can still share a meal, laugh together, help a stranger, enjoy a ride, watch their children play, listen to a story, show compassion, stand beside someone in a hard moment, and spend a beautiful day together. **The goal is not forced agreement — it's restoring some human connection.**

## Handling Backlash

Adam expects criticism eventually — from people who think PromiseLand is too Christian, and from Christians who think it isn't Christian enough. The organization should not constantly change direction based on whichever group is angry that week. When criticism comes, return to the Founder Compass below: serve families, tell the story we believe, love our neighbors, treat people with dignity, build something beautiful, create places where families connect, keep the door open. **PromiseLand does not have to participate in every cultural fight.**

## What This Means for Park Design

Christian storytelling can remain direct and meaningful, but attractions should not be designed primarily around condemnation, "us versus them" storytelling, portraying outsiders as enemies, mocking other beliefs, trapping guests in sermons, or publicly shaming anyone. Emphasize instead: grace, hope, forgiveness, sacrifice, courage, restoration, family, love, belonging, compassion, service, redemption, wonder. **The strongest Christian storytelling should make someone want to think about the message, not feel forced to listen to it.**

## What This Means for Employees

A future PromiseLand hospitality philosophy should be built from this vision. Core points: a guest doesn't need to share PromiseLand's beliefs to receive excellent treatment; employees should never debate guests about religion, sexuality, politics, or culture while working; employees represent PromiseLand primarily through kindness, professionalism, patience, service, competence, hospitality, and respect. This doesn't mean employees can't hold personal beliefs — it means hospitality comes first while serving guests. **Any future employment policy built from this must comply with applicable law — LEGAL / HR PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED.**

## What This Means for Marketing

PromiseLand should not hide being Christian, but marketing's primary message should never be "this park is not for you." The intended feeling: *"This is the story we believe. Come experience it with us. You are welcome here."* PromiseLand should be confident enough in its faith that it doesn't need hostility to prove its convictions.

## The Bigger Picture

The park is part of a larger idea: people helping people, families reconnecting, communities becoming a little less hostile toward each other, children experiencing wonder, parents making memories, people encountering stories about grace, hope, and Jesus in an environment where they feel welcomed rather than attacked. Adam's hope: guests leave thinking not only "that was an amazing theme park" but perhaps also "I felt something there," "I thought about something differently," "my family needed that day together," "those people actually cared about us." **What happens spiritually after that is not something PromiseLand controls.**

## Founder Compass

- PromiseLand will not hide its faith.
- Every person who enters will be treated with dignity.
- We serve before we argue.
- We share our beliefs without treating ourselves as superior.
- We will hold conviction without cruelty.
- We will tell stories about grace, hope, love, and redemption.
- We do not need everyone to agree with us to welcome them.
- We want families and people who may disagree to experience something beautiful together.
- The door stays open.
