# Promise Promenade

**FOUNDER DIRECTION — UNDER EVALUATION. CONCEPT — NOT YET APPROVED.** A new working concept: a free-access, non-ticketed dining/retail/entertainment district in [[Promiseland Park]]'s arrival sequence, between parking and the [[Park Master Plan|PromiseLand]] gates. Guests can visit without buying a park ticket. Not official canon — nothing here is in [[Decision Log]].

**Founder's core directive, in his own framing:** *do not make Promise Promenade into a generic shopping center. It should financially function as commercial real estate while emotionally functioning as the first chapter of PromiseLand.*

## Concept
Not inside the ticketed park. Free-access coffee, breakfast, restaurants, dessert, retail (local businesses and national brands), live music, a small entertainment stage, fountains, landscaping, family gathering areas, nighttime dining, and seasonal events — functioning somewhat like a destination entertainment district attached to a major theme park, but with an original PromiseLand identity, not a copy of any existing property. Because it doesn't require a ticket, it can generate revenue even when guests aren't entering the park.

## Arrival Sequence (working concept)
Vehicle Arrival → Parking Structure + Surface Parking → Immersive Pedestrian Walkways/Bridges → **Promise Promenade** → Gathering Forecourt → Ticketing/Security → PromiseLand Entry Gates → [[Hope Street]] → [[Promise Plaza]]/land connections. See [[Park Master Plan]] for the full arrival-sequence writeup.

## Original Architectural Design Standards (working concept)
Promise Promenade should not read as "parking lot → generic coffee chain → generic fast food → gate." Outside brands may retain recognizable identity, but building exterior, materials, landscaping, lighting, signage scale, patios, awnings, outdoor furniture, and the public realm must conform to the PromiseLand arrival environment — immersion begins before ticket entry, not at the gate.

**Recommended Phase 1 target: MEDIUM.** Large enough to function as genuine standalone commercial real estate and carry the "first chapter" emotional weight, without ballooning acreage/capital ahead of real attendance data — consistent with COMPACT PREMIUM's "don't overbuild Phase 1" principle. SMALL is the fallback if capital is tight; LARGE is a natural Phase 1B/2 expansion path, since it sits outside the ticketed park and can grow without touching Phase 1's layout.

## Third-Party Tenant / Development Strategy — six structures researched

No named brand is stated as interested in PromiseLand — all names below are illustrative of tenant *types* only.

**A. Ground lease.** PromiseLand owns the land; tenant leases a pad/building site long-term and may fund/build its own structure; PromiseLand receives land rent. **MARKET BENCHMARK:** typical ground rent runs **6–11% of unimproved land's fair market value/year** (urban 7–8%, rural/suburban 5–7%); alternate rule of thumb: ground rent ≈ 10–20% of total occupancy cost. Terms can run up to 99 years. Escalation is usually fixed bumps, CPI, fair-market-value resets, or a percentage-rent kicker.

**B. Build-to-suit.** A commercial developer constructs a location for a committed tenant, then leases the finished building. **MARKET BENCHMARK:** typically 10–20 year lease terms given the building's tenant-specific design; developer bears construction risk against a locked-in tenant.

**C. PromiseLand builds shell, tenant funds interior.** **MARKET BENCHMARK (tenant improvement allowances, 2026):** retail **$10–250/SF** (basic $40–90, mid-tier $90–180, premium $150–300); restaurant **$100–480/SF** (varies heavily by service format). LA-market landlord-offered allowances specifically run $20–80/SF depending on submarket/tenant credit — note this is the *offered allowance*, not necessarily full construction cost.

**D. Master commercial developer.** A third-party CRE developer finances and builds most of Promise Promenade and leases space to tenants. **Real structural precedent found:** the Glendale Entertainment District (CO) — a master-planned mixed-use retail/entertainment/commercial center with cinema, live-concert venues, outdoor plazas, dining, a hotel, and two parking structures — a close structural analog to this concept. Real financing tools used for large entertainment districts: tax increment financing (TIF), infrastructure bonds, opportunity zones, special assessment districts, blended CMBS/life-insurance permanent loans, and ground-lease/profit-sharing arrangements for adjacent parcels.

**E. Mixed model.** Anchor tenants/developers fund their own buildings while PromiseLand controls common areas, landscaping, entertainment, architecture, and guest experience via the design standards above.

**F. Base rent + percentage rent.** **MARKET BENCHMARK:** standard structure is fixed base rent plus a percentage of gross sales above a negotiated breakpoint. **Restaurants/cafés typically 6–10% percentage rent**; retail generally ranges 1–10% (low-margin/high-volume tenants at the low end, high-margin retail at the high end). Breakpoint is either "natural" (annual base rent ÷ percentage rate) or "artificial" (negotiated off projected sales). **NNN/CAM specifics and Inland Empire-specific retail/restaurant rent benchmarks: NOT RESEARCHED this pass** (search capacity exhausted) — flagged honestly rather than filled with an unsourced number; PROFESSIONAL VALIDATION REQUIRED / MARKET BENCHMARK RESEARCH REQUIRED before relying on any CAM figure.
